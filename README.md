## 1. Introduction

Adverse drug reactions (ADRs) are unintended and harmful responses associated with the use of medicines at normally used doses. Pharmacovigilance is the science and activities concerned with detecting, assessing, understanding, and preventing adverse effects or other medicine-related problems.

The thalidomide tragedy of the late 1950s and early 1960s highlighted the importance of monitoring medicine safety, particularly during pregnancy, and contributed to the development of modern drug safety monitoring systems.

## 2. Types of Adverse Drug Reactions

The tutorial discusses several mechanisms and categories of drug reactions:

- **Pharmacological toxicity:** Excessive pharmacological effects, such as hypotension caused by excessive blood pressure reduction.
- **Pathological toxicity:** Tissue or organ damage, such as liver injury associated with paracetamol overdose.
- **Genotoxicity:** Damage to genetic material, which may occur with certain medicines, including some anticancer agents.
- **Allergic reactions:** Immune-mediated reactions that may present as rashes, bronchospasm, or severe systemic reactions.
- **Idiosyncratic reactions:** Unusual or unexpected responses that occur in particular individuals.
- **Pharmacogenetic reactions:** Differences in drug response associated with genetic variation. For example, primaquine can cause haemolysis in individuals with glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase (G6PD) deficiency.
- **Teratogenic effects:** Effects on embryonic or fetal development caused by certain medicines during pregnancy.

## 3. Hypersensitivity Reactions

Drug-related hypersensitivity reactions are classified into four major types:

1. **Type I:** Immediate, IgE-mediated reactions that can include urticaria and anaphylaxis.
2. **Type II:** Antibody-mediated reactions involving cell injury, such as drug-induced haemolytic anaemia.
3. **Type III:** Immune-complex-mediated reactions, which may produce serum sickness-like manifestations.
4. **Type IV:** Delayed, T-cell-mediated reactions, including certain drug-related skin reactions.

Recognizing these patterns helps healthcare professionals identify suspected medicine-related reactions and assess their potential seriousness.

## 4. Classification by Onset and Severity

The tutorial describes ADRs according to the time of onset and clinical severity.

Severity may range from minor reactions that require little or no intervention to serious or life-threatening reactions that require urgent treatment. In pharmacovigilance, seriousness is assessed separately using outcomes such as death, life-threatening events, hospitalization, disability, congenital anomalies, or other medically important events.

The timing of a reaction and its clinical severity help guide documentation, assessment, and follow-up.

## 5. Pharmacovigilance and Causality Assessment

Causality assessment evaluates whether a medicine may have caused a reported reaction. Important considerations include:

- The temporal relationship between medicine exposure and reaction onset.
- Whether the reaction is consistent with the medicine's known safety profile.
- Alternative explanations, including underlying disease and concomitant medicines.
- The clinical course after withdrawal or dose reduction, when available.
- Relevant laboratory findings and other supporting evidence.

The tutorial introduces the WHO–UMC causality assessment system and the Naranjo algorithm as methods for evaluating suspected drug-related reactions.

A suspected association should not automatically be considered proof of causation.

## 6. Adverse Drug Reaction Reporting in India

The Pharmacovigilance Programme of India (PvPI) supports the monitoring of medicine safety in India. Suspected ADRs can be reported by healthcare professionals and consumers through applicable reporting channels.

A suspected ADR reporting form generally records the following information:

- Patient information, using appropriate confidentiality safeguards.
- Description of the suspected reaction and its onset and outcome.
- Suspected medicine details, including dose, route, frequency, indication, and treatment dates.
- Concomitant medicines and relevant medical history.
- Action taken with the suspected medicine.
- Whether the reaction recurred after re-exposure, if this information is available.
- Seriousness criteria, relevant investigations, and the reporter's details.

Only available and verified information should be recorded. Missing details should be identified for follow-up rather than invented.

## 7. Role of the Pharmacovigilance Professional

A pharmacovigilance professional contributes to patient safety by identifying potential ADRs, collecting accurate case information, documenting suspected reactions, assessing seriousness and causality, and supporting timely reporting.

Clear documentation, confidentiality, accurate timelines, and evidence-based assessment are essential components of good pharmacovigilance practice.

## 8. Conclusion

The tutorial explains the fundamentals of adverse drug reactions, their mechanisms and clinical manifestations, the principles of causality assessment, and the process of suspected ADR reporting. These concepts support the systematic identification, documentation, and evaluation of medicine-related safety concerns.

The key practical lesson is that a suspected ADR must be documented accurately and assessed using the available clinical evidence. Reporting suspected reactions contributes to the ongoing evaluation of medicine safety and the protection of public health.
